The problem

Learning to listen

Kia ora, I’m Lucia. I’m originally from Chengdu, China, and now based in Christchurch, building my career in data.

My background is in psychology. It taught me to approach people with curiosity rather than judgement. Later, in Apple technical support, I learned that people needed more than a technical answer. They needed to feel heard and understand what to do next. I learned to listen, explain things simply, and stay calm when the answer wasn’t obvious.

Finding my voice

My job was to help people work through uncertainty. Then I moved to New Zealand, and I was the one feeling unsure. In classroom discussions, I understood the topic. I had my own ideas. But I wasn’t sure I could express them clearly in English. So I stayed quiet.

Later, for a project presentation, I prepared thoroughly. I made sure I understood the material and practised explaining it aloud. The presentation went well, and the encouraging response gave me more confidence to share my ideas.

Connecting data to everyday life

As I studied data science, I also began to see my earlier experiences differently. Psychology, technical support, and data weren’t separate chapters. They shared something important: asking questions, understanding problems, and making complex information useful.

In one project, I explored antimicrobial resistance in a Christchurch river. I worked with complex data from different sources, looking for patterns and possible connections. One observation stayed with me: after heavy rain, some chemical measures linked to poorer water quality seemed to rise.

That alone couldn’t tell us whether the river was safe for swimming. But it made me think about the warning signs beside a river. It made the work feel closer to everyday life. The patterns I was studying were connected to a river people live beside and use.

What I bring to data work

Looking back across these experiences, I can see what I want to bring to data work: empathy, careful analysis, and clear communication. I want to help people understand complex problems and feel more confident about what to do next.

If that resonates with you, I’d love to connect over coffee and explore the human side of data and technology.
